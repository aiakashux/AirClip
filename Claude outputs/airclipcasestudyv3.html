The problem

A feature I never thought about, until it was gone

For two years I carried an iPhone, an Android, and a MacBook. Copy something on the iPhone, paste it on the Mac. It just worked. I never set it up and I never thought about it.

The Android sat outside all of that. I noticed, but the iPhone was always there to fall back on.

Then I lost the iPhone, and the Android became my only phone.

Fifteen seconds each time. Small enough to ignore, often enough to wear on you.

You get pulled awayYou open WhatsApp to send yourself a link, and someone has messaged you about something that matters. A minute later you have forgotten what you copied.
Codes run outA login code lasts thirty seconds. The detour eats most of it.
It piles up in a chatAddresses, keys, and codes end up sitting in a message thread forever.

Nobody would ever report this as a bug. Too small to complain about, too normal to notice.

What I found

It was already solved, just not for people like me

Before building anything I went looking. Reddit, a few maker communities. People had solved this, and some of it was good work.

Then I tried to use it. Step one was usually clone this repo. Then run something in Terminal, find your device's IP address, and type it into a file on both machines. Again every time the WiFi changed.

I have a computer science background and I still had to look things up. Almost nobody I know would get through that. The easy apps had the opposite problem: simple to install, and your clipboard lives on their servers.

The turning point

My first version had a server. Losing my phone is what killed it

I built the obvious version first. A small server in the middle, both devices connect to it, clips pass through. It worked fine.

Two things nagged at me. I would be paying for that server forever. And I would be asking strangers to trust me about how I ran it.

Then the real problem landed. If any device can reach that server from anywhere, so can a phone I no longer own.

I had just lost a phone. I was building the exact thing that had scared me.

So I deleted it and wrote one rule instead:

Devices only talk when they are on the same network.

Same WiFi. Same hotspot. Same room, basically. If a device is not near me it gets nothing, and there is no account for a lost phone to log back into.

The cost is real. Nothing waits. If my phone is on mobile data and I copy something on the Mac, that clip never arrives.

What I built

Small enough to forget it is running

On the Mac it lives in the menu bar. Click it, see your recent clips, click one, it is copied. Most days that is the entire app.

The full window has three tabs. Clips is your history, labelled with the device each one came from. Devices shows what is paired and whether it is online. Settings holds the rest.

Devices matters more here than in a normal app. Being online is the whole condition for this working, so when nothing syncs that screen has to say why in plain words instead of spinning.

Android does the same, shaped for a phone, with a share button and a home screen widget for sending on purpose.

What is coming

It started as text. Now it handles links, images, and colours. Bigger files and video are next, and they need a progress bar and a way to cancel, so that is its own problem. Windows and Linux after that.

Privacy and security

What actually happens when you copy something

Your Mac notices you copied something and checks it against a few rules. If it passes, it locks the content and sends it straight to your phone across your own WiFi. Your phone unlocks it and puts it on your clipboard.

It never touches the internet. There is no company in the middle, and that includes me.

Some things do not move on their own

Passwords, keys, and card numbers are blocked before they leave your device.

Login codes ask you first instead. That is on purpose, because moving a login code between a phone and a laptop is the main reason anyone wants this at all. Blocking those would have broken the app. Every rule can be changed.

Three ways to run it

Automatic sends everything. Manual sends only what you pick. Paused stops all of it. Manual is there so that when automatic feels like too much, the answer is turning it down instead of deleting the app.

The risks, and the trade

The one to think about is someone else on your WiFi. Devices have to be paired first and clips are locked before sending, so being on the network is not enough to read anything. Still, a shared office network is different from your home, and that is worth knowing.

Lose a device and it cannot pull anything unless it is back on your network. That was the point.

No server means nothing waits for you when a device is away. It also means there is nothing to hack, no account to leak, and no company that could be sold or pressured into changing its mind later.

What broke

Taking out the server did not remove the hard part. It moved it into the two apps.

A server keeps score. It knows who is connected and what arrived. Without one the two devices work that out themselves, which is fine until a laptop wakes from sleep or a phone changes WiFi.

I got that wrong. My Mac sometimes does not reconnect after waking, and quitting and reopening is the only fix. That is the kind of bug that gets a small tool deleted rather than reported, and the decision I am proudest of is what caused it.

What I cannot see

Anything about how it gets used. There is no tracking in it, because the whole promise is that it does not report back. So I do not know if anyone uses manual mode, or where setup loses people. A real product would have to price anonymous measuring against that promise. I have not had to make that call.

What I am least sure about

Same network only means someone copying on their office laptop and wanting it on their phone on the train gets nothing. That is not a rare person. I cut them out on principle, and the only research behind it was me.

What I learned

I set out to save four steps

01

The thing that started it is not what shaped it

I began because pasting between my devices was annoying. Every decision that mattered came from a different question: what happens the next time I lose a device.

02

Taking something out moves the work somewhere else

I thought I was removing a server. I was handing its job to two apps that now have to agree without one, and the reconnection bug is the bill.

03

Not being an engineer helped

I could not build the clever version, so every rule had to be one I could say in a sentence. Same network means we are together. If it looks like a password, it does not move. Product rules first, technical ones second.

It saves me four steps, a few dozen times a day. What I actually care about is that nothing I copy ever leaves the room.
